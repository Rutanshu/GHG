How to use these factors

Spend-based

You multiply money by an economic input-output intensity. Use it when you know what you paid but not what you received — long-tail suppliers, services, early screening years.

kgCO2e = spend (in the factor's price-year currency) × factor (kgCO2e / currency unit)

Two traps. First, price is a poor proxy for physical impact: a premium supplier with a cleaner process looks worse because it charges more. Second, inflation. If your spend is in 2025 dollars and the factor's price_year is 2022, deflate your spend to 2022 first, or you will overstate emissions by the intervening inflation. Every spend-based record here carries a price_year for exactly that reason.

Weight-based and activity-based

You multiply a physical quantity — kilograms, kWh, litres, tonne-kilometres, passenger-kilometres — by a physical intensity. Always better than spend-based where the data exists, because it responds to what you actually bought rather than what it cost.

kgCO2e = quantity × factor (kgCO2e / physical unit)

Supplier-specific

Your supplier gives you their own verified product footprint. This replaces both of the above and is the only route that rewards a supplier for decarbonising. The register cannot hold these — they are yours.

The data quality ladder

Supplier-specific → physical average-data → hybrid → spend-based. Record which rung each line of your inventory sits on. A footprint built entirely on spend-based factors is a screening exercise, not a measurement, and should be described that way.

Double counting, the three usual places

  • 3.1 vs 3.3. Cradle-to-gate purchased-goods factors often already include the fuel supply chain. Do not add well-to-tank again on top.
  • 3.4 vs 3.9. A freight leg belongs to whoever pays for it. Upstream if you pay, downstream if your customer does.
  • Scope 1 vs 3.3. Combustion is scope 1; extracting, refining, and delivering that fuel is 3.3. Different factors, both needed, never the same number twice.

Scope 2, twice

Report a location-based figure (grid average where you consume) and a market-based figure (your contractual instruments). They are not alternatives; the GHG Protocol asks for both.

3.15 is not a lookup

Financed emissions are an attribution calculation — your share of an investee's emissions based on outstanding amount over enterprise value — not a factor you multiply spend by. Read PCAF before touching that row.

Adding a source

Data lives in data/factors/*.json — one file per source dataset, listed in data/index.json. Every record must validate against data/schema.json. The etl/ folder holds the Python parser used for each dataset currently loaded (Defra, EPA Hub, eGRID) — add a new agency by writing a parser that reads the publisher's own spreadsheet or API and emits records in the schema shape, then register the output file in data/index.json. Three rules enforced by etl/combine.py:

  • A record with value_status: "verified" must have a real number, read mechanically from the cited file — not typed in — and a source_page_or_table precise enough for someone else to find it.
  • Any spend-based record must declare its price_year.
  • Records from licensed datasets (ecoinvent, IEA) may carry the citation and units but not the value. Keep the licence intact.

Agencies known but not yet loaded — IPCC EFDB, ADEME, Australia DCCEEW (currently unreachable from this build's network), and others — are listed with "populated": false in data/sources.json as the next places to extend this register.
